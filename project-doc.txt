ABSTRACT 
 
Timely access to compatible blood is critical during medical emergencies. Hospitals frequently 
depend on phone calls, social-media appeals, messaging groups, and manually maintained 
records to locate donors, which consumes valuable time. To address this problem, this project 
presents BloodLink, a web-based emergency blood donor finder and blood request 
management platform that connects donors, hospitals, blood banks, and administrators on a 
single system. 
In BloodLink, a hospital creates a blood request. The system identifies eligible and available 
donors with compatible blood groups in the hospital's city using stored donor and hospital 
coordinates and the Haversine distance formula. The search begins with a small radius and is 
progressively expanded (2, 5, 10, 15, and 20 km) when no suitable donor is found. Matching 
donors are ranked using a priority score that considers factors such as distance, recent donation 
information, recent alcohol consumption, eligibility, and availability, and are notified through 
the in-app notification system. Donors respond to the request, the hospital selects a suitable 
donor response, and the completed donation is recorded in the donation history. BloodLink 
does not track donors continuously; it relies on stored location information. 
The system is implemented as a three-tier web application using React.js for the frontend, 
Node.js with Express.js for the REST API backend, and PostgreSQL as the primary database. 
Authentication is based on JSON Web Tokens (JWT) with a four-hour expiry and bcrypt 
password hashing, and access to functionality is controlled through role-based authorization 
for the DONOR, HOSPITAL, BLOOD_BANK, and ADMIN roles. Database indexing, 
bounding-box pre-filtering, and load testing were considered to evaluate the performance of 
the nearby-donor search. Features such as SMS and push notifications, Redis-based caching 
and queues, request expiration, blood bank inventory management, and machine-learning-
based prediction are identified as future enhancements and are not part of the present 
implementation. 
Keywords: blood donation, emergency blood request, donor matching, Haversine distance, 
PostgreSQL, Node.js, React.js, role-based access control.

1. INTRODUCTION 
Blood is one of the most essential resources in healthcare and is required for surgeries, accident 
and trauma cases, childbirth-related complications, cancer treatment, and various other medical 
emergencies. The World Health Organization (WHO) emphasizes the importance of 
maintaining a safe and adequate supply of blood to meet the needs of patients requiring 
transfusion. During an emergency, timely access to the required blood group can be critical for 
effective medical treatment and patient survival. Therefore, ensuring the availability of 
compatible blood at the right time and place is an important challenge for healthcare systems. 
Traditional approaches to locating blood donors depend on blood banks, donor directories, 
social-media appeals, messaging groups, or personal contacts. These approaches require 
considerable manual effort and consume valuable time in critical situations. Although several 
online blood donation platforms exist, some of them concentrate mainly on donor registration 
and request listing rather than on coordinating the complete request-to-donation workflow. 
With the availability of web technologies and location information, it is possible to identify 
donors near a hospital by comparing stored coordinates and calculating approximate distances. 
Matching donors by blood group, eligibility, availability, and distance can reduce the manual 
effort involved in finding suitable donors. 
This project presents BloodLink – Real Time Emergency Blood Donor Finder System, a 
web application through which hospitals create blood requests and donors respond to them. 
When a request is created, BloodLink identifies compatible, eligible, and available donors in 
the hospital's city using stored coordinates and the Haversine distance formula, expands the 
search radius progressively when required, ranks the candidates using a priority score, and 
notifies them through in-app notifications. Donors respond to the request, the hospital selects 
a suitable donor response, and the completed donation is recorded in the donation history. The 
application is developed using React.js, Node.js, Express.js, and PostgreSQL, with JWT-based 
authentication, bcrypt password hashing, and role-based access control for donors, hospitals, 
blood banks, and administrators. 
In this report, the term "real time" in the project title refers to the immediate identification and 
notification of matching donors when a request is created. It does not imply continuous GPS 
tracking of donors: BloodLink uses stored donor and hospital location information and does 
not track donors in the background. Features that are planned but not yet implemented, such as 
SMS and push notifications, Redis-based caching, request expiration, blood bank inventory 
management, and machine-learning-based prediction, are described only as future 
enhancements in Section 6.2. 
1.1 Motivation 
The motivation behind BloodLink arises from the need for timely access to blood during 
medical emergencies. Many patients require immediate transfusions because of accidents, 
surgeries, childbirth complications, and severe illnesses. Despite the presence of blood banks 
2 
 
and donor databases, locating a compatible and available donor within a short period remains 
a challenge. 
In many cases, hospitals rely on manual phone calls, social-media posts, or personal networks 
to find donors. These methods are time-consuming and may not reach eligible donors quickly. 
Some existing platforms also provide limited support for location-based matching and for 
tracking the progress of a request after donors have been contacted. 
BloodLink is motivated by the aim of centralising emergency blood requests and donor 
coordination in a single web platform, so that hospitals can reach nearby compatible donors, 
donors can respond easily, and the request can be followed through to completion and recorded. 
1.2 Problem Statement 
During medical emergencies, patients and hospitals often face significant challenges in locating 
compatible blood donors within the limited time available. Existing arrangements frequently 
rely on manual communication or static donor lists for donor identification. As a result, 
considerable time may be spent searching for eligible donors, which can delay critical medical 
treatment. 
An efficient system is required to quickly identify nearby compatible donors, notify them, 
record their responses, maintain donor eligibility and availability information, and provide 
secure management of blood requests. Such a system should improve coordination among 
donors, hospitals, blood banks, and administrators while supporting timely access to blood 
during emergencies. 
1.3 Problem Objectives 
The main objectives of the BloodLink system are: 
• To develop a web-based application for centralised management of emergency blood 
requests and donor coordination. 
• To identify compatible, eligible, and available donors near a requesting hospital using 
blood-group compatibility, city, stored coordinates, and Haversine distance with a 
progressively expanding search radius. 
• To rank candidate donors using a priority score and notify them through in-app 
notifications. 
• To allow donors to respond to blood requests and hospitals to select a donor response 
and complete the request. 
• To maintain donor availability and eligibility information and a record of completed 
donations. 
• To provide secure role-based access for donors, hospitals, blood banks, and 
administrators using JWT authentication and bcrypt password hashing. 
• To evaluate the performance of the APIs and the nearby-donor search using database 
indexing and load testing.

2. LITERATURE SURVEY 
Blood donation management has evolved with the advancement of web technologies, mobile 
applications, cloud computing, and location-based services. Several researchers have proposed 
systems to improve donor registration, blood inventory management, emergency 
communication, and donor–recipient coordination. This chapter reviews selected contributions 
and identifies the limitations that motivate the development of BloodLink. The studies 
discussed in this chapter are supported by their respective original publications and are cited 
using the corresponding reference numbers. Complete bibliographic details of all cited studies 
are provided in the References section. 
2.1 Existing Work 
2.1.1 Mobile Cloud-Based Blood Donation Framework (2014) 
Mostafa et al. [1] proposed a mobile cloud-based blood donation framework that enables 
centralised management of donor information and communication among hospitals, blood 
banks, and donors. The study demonstrated the use of cloud computing for providing scalable 
and accessible blood donation services. 
2.1.2 GPS-Based Blood Donor Search System (2019) 
Swetha and Bhavani [2] developed a GPS-based blood donor search system that uses location-
based services to identify nearby compatible donors during emergencies. Their work 
highlighted the importance of geospatial information in improving donor discovery. 
2.1.3 Mobile Blood Donation Applications (2023) 
Li et al. [3] presented a systematic review of mobile blood donation applications, discussing 
the role of GPS, notification systems, and mobile technologies in supporting donor 
participation and emergency blood management. 
2.1.4 Blood Hub: Blood Delivery Management System (2024) 
The Blood Hub system [4] describes a Google Maps-based blood delivery management 
platform intended to improve coordination between blood banks and hospitals, illustrating the 
use of location-aware technology in blood logistics. 
2.1.5 AI-Based Blood Donation Management System (2025) 
An IEEE conference paper presented at ICSSAS (2025) [5] proposed an AI-based blood 
donation management system using the MERN stack and Deep Neural Networks (DNN) to 
predict blood demand and support the planning of blood donation activities. 
2.1.6 Rakt Setu: MERN-Based Blood Donation Platform (2026) 
Rakt Setu [6] is described as a MERN-stack-based blood donation platform with geospatial 
search for donor registration and emergency blood request management. The study is an IEEE 
conference paper published in the 9th International Conference on Inventive Computation 
Technologies (ICICT), 2026, and is cited as Reference [6] in the References section. 
5 
 
2.2 Limitations of Existing Work 
The reviewed systems have contributed to donor registration, blood inventory management, 
emergency blood requests, donor communication, and location-based donor identification. 
However, several challenges remain in providing a unified and efficient platform for complete 
blood request management. These gaps motivate the development of BloodLink: 
• End-to-end request management: Existing systems often focus on donor registration, 
donor discovery, or inventory management, with limited support for the complete 
workflow of request creation, donor response, selection, completion, and donation 
history. BloodLink integrates these activities into a structured workflow. 
• Efficient donor matching: Identifying suitable donors requires considering blood-
group compatibility, eligibility, availability, and location. BloodLink combines these 
factors to improve donor identification and prioritization. 
• Progressive location-based search: A fixed search radius may fail to find suitable 
donors during emergencies. BloodLink progressively expands the search radius from 2 
km to 20 km to improve donor availability. 
• Timely communication: BloodLink provides in-app notifications for relevant blood 
requests, helping improve communication between hospitals and potential donors. 
• Role-based coordination: BloodLink connects donors, hospitals, blood banks, and 
administrators through role-based access, allowing each stakeholder to perform 
appropriate operations. 
• Centralized management: The platform reduces dependence on phone calls, 
messaging groups, and manual records by providing a centralized system for managing 
blood requests, donor responses, and donation history. 
Overall, BloodLink aims to provide an integrated platform that combines blood request 
management, donor matching, location-based search, notifications, role-based coordination, 
and donation history to improve the efficiency of emergency blood coordination.

3.3 Functional Requirements 
Functional requirements describe the services that BloodLink provides in its current 
implementation. 
3.3.1 User Registration and Login 
• Users shall be able to register and log in. The system supports the roles DONOR, 
HOSPITAL, BLOOD_BANK, and ADMIN.

• Each user shall have a unique email address and phone number. 
• Passwords shall be hashed using bcrypt before storage. 
• On successful login, the system shall issue a JWT containing the user identification and 
role. The token expires after four hours. 
3.3.2 Role-Based Access and Dashboards 
• Protected API operations shall be accessible only to users whose role is authorised for 
them. 
• Role-specific dashboards shall provide users with information and operations relevant 
to their assigned role, including blood requests, donor/request responses, donation 
history, notifications, and other relevant project activities. 
3.3.3 Donor and Hospital Profiles 
• A donor profile shall store blood group, city, eligibility status, availability for requests, 
latitude, longitude, recent alcohol consumption information, and donation history. 
• A hospital profile shall store hospital details, latitude, longitude, verification status, and 
a unique registration number. 
3.3.4 Blood Request Management 
• Hospitals shall be able to create blood requests specifying the required blood group, 
number of units, urgency, location-related information, and other relevant request 
details. 
• Users shall be able to view their own requests, the list of open requests, and a request 
by its identifier. 
• A blood request shall have one of the statuses: OPEN, ACCEPTED, COMPLETED, 
or CANCELLED. 
3.3.5 Blood Group Compatibility and Nearby Donor Matching 
• The system shall identify donors whose blood group is compatible with the requested 
blood group. 
• Only eligible and available donors in the relevant city shall be considered for matching. 
• The distance between the hospital and each donor shall be calculated using their stored 
latitude and longitude values and the Haversine formula, following a bounding-box pre-
filter. 
• The search shall progressively expand through the configured radius levels of 2 km, 5 
km, 10 km, 15 km, and 20 km when no suitable donor is found. 
• The system shall use stored location information for donor matching and shall not 
continuously track donor locations. 
8 
 
3.3.6 Donor Priority Ranking 
• Matching donors shall be ranked using a priority score that considers factors such as 
distance, eligibility, availability, recent donation information, and recent alcohol 
consumption. 
• The priority score shall be used to prioritize suitable donors, with donor suitability and 
proximity contributing to the ranking. 
3.3.7 Donor Responses and Selection 
• A donor shall be able to respond to a blood request. A response is linked to one blood 
request and one donor, and a donor can have only one response per request. 
• A response shall have one of the statuses PENDING, ACCEPTED, or REJECTED. 
• The hospital shall be able to select a donor response for its request. 
3.3.8 Request Completion and Donation History 
• The hospital shall be able to mark a request as completed. 
• The system shall record the completed donation in the donation history. 
3.3.9 In-App Notifications 
• The system shall create in-app notifications for events such as new blood requests and 
donor-related request notifications, and shall support test notifications. 
3.4 Non-Functional Requirements 
Non-functional requirements define the quality attributes of BloodLink. 
3.4.1 Performance 
• Nearby-donor matching should respond quickly. PostgreSQL indexing, bounding-box 
filtering before the Haversine calculation, and query optimisation are used for this 
purpose. 
• API and matching performance has been evaluated using load testing with Autocannon; 
the measured results are reported in Section 5.3. 
3.4.2 Security 
• Passwords are stored as bcrypt hashes. 
• Authentication uses JWT with a four-hour expiry, and role-based authorisation restricts 
access to authorised roles. 
• Configuration values such as secrets and database settings are kept in environment 
variables (dotenv), and CORS is enabled for the API. 
• Password reset, email verification, rate limiting, and audit logging are not part of the 
current implementation.

3.4.3 Usability 
• The application provides a web-based interface with role-specific functionality that can 
be accessed through modern web browsers. 
• The interface is designed to allow users to perform operations relevant to their roles, 
such as managing blood requests, donor responses, profiles, notifications, and related

3.4.4 Reliability and Data Integrity 
• Application data is stored in PostgreSQL, which serves as the primary source of truth

• Database constraints, including unique constraints, foreign-key relationships, and

• Unique constraints are applied to important fields such as user email, phone number,

3.4.5 Maintainability 
• The backend follows a modular Express.js structure with separate configuration,

• The system is designed with horizontal scaling in mind, allowing additional server

• Redis-based caching, queues, rate limiting, and other distributed mechanisms are 
proposed as future enhancements and are not part of the current implementation. 
3.4.7 Compatibility 
• The system is designed to be compatible with modern web browsers, including Google 
Chrome, Microsoft Edge, and Mozilla Firefox. 
• The web-based architecture allows users to access the application without requiring a 
dedicated desktop application.

4. PROPOSED SYSTEM DESIGN 
4.1 Proposed Methods 
BloodLink is designed as an emergency blood donor management platform that connects 
hospitals with suitable nearby donors. The system follows a modular architecture consisting of 
authentication and authorisation, donor and hospital management, blood request management, 
nearby donor matching, donor response management, donation history, and in-app 
notifications. 
When a hospital creates a blood request, the request is stored with the status OPEN. The system 
determines the compatible donor blood groups and considers only donors who are eligible and 
available in the hospital's city. A bounding box around the hospital coordinates removes distant 
donors cheaply, and the Haversine formula then calculates the approximate distance of the 
remaining donors. The search starts with a small radius and, if no suitable donor is found, is 
expanded progressively (2, 5, 10, 15, and 20 km). 
Matching donors are ranked using a priority score based on factors such as distance, recent 
donation information, recent alcohol consumption, eligibility, and availability, and in-app 
notifications are created for them. Donors respond to the request (response status PENDING). 
The hospital selects a donor response (ACCEPTED) and later completes the request, after 
which the donation is recorded in the donation history. 
Location is handled using stored coordinates of donors and hospitals. BloodLink does not 
perform continuous background GPS tracking of donors. Access to all protected operations is 
controlled using JWT authentication and role-based authorisation. 
The main methods used are: 
• Blood-group compatibility filtering 
• Donor eligibility and availability filtering 
• Bounding-box pre-filtering and Haversine distance calculation 
• Progressive search-radius expansion 
• Donor priority scoring 
• Donor response and selection workflow 
• In-app notifications 
• JWT authentication and role-based access control 
The statuses used in the request and response workflow are summarised below.

BloodLink uses a three-tier architecture. The React.js frontend communicates with the 
Express.js backend through REST APIs. Requests pass through the routing layer and the 
authentication and authorisation layer to the controllers, which implement the business logic 
and access PostgreSQL through a connection pool created with the pg library.

5. IMPLEMENTATION AND TESTING 
5.1 Implementation 
BloodLink is implemented as a full-stack web application. The frontend is developed with 
React.js and provides interfaces for donors, hospitals, blood banks, and administrators 
according to their roles. The backend is developed with Node.js and Express.js using 
CommonJS modules and exposes REST APIs. PostgreSQL is the primary database and is 
accessed through a connection pool created with the pg library. Environment-specific 
configuration is loaded using dotenv, and CORS is enabled so that the frontend can access the 
API. 
5.1.1 Authentication and Authorisation 
Users register and log in through the authentication APIs. Passwords are hashed with bcrypt 
before storage. After a successful login, the server issues a JWT that contains the user 
identification and role and expires after four hours. Middleware verifies the token and checks 
the user's role before allowing access to protected routes. 
5.1.2 Blood Request and Response Module 
Hospitals create blood requests, which are stored with the status OPEN. Donors respond to a 
request through the respond API, creating a response with the status PENDING; a donor can 
respond to a given request only once. The hospital selects a response through the select API 
and completes the request through the complete API, after which the donation is recorded in 
the donation history. 
5.1.3 Nearby Donor Matching 
The nearby-donor API accepts a search radius. The system filters donors by blood-group 
compatibility, city, eligibility, and availability, applies a bounding box around the hospital 
coordinates, and calculates the Haversine distance for the remaining donors. The radius is 
expanded progressively when no suitable donor is found. A priority score is calculated for the 
matching donors using distance, recent donation information, recent alcohol consumption, 
eligibility, and availability. Donor locations are taken from stored profile information; no 
continuous tracking is performed. 
5.1.4 Notification Module 
BloodLink uses an in-app notification system. Notifications are created for events such as new 
blood requests and donor-related request notifications, and they can be viewed by the recipient 
within the application. SMS and push notifications are not part of the current implementation.

6. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE ENHANCEMENTS 
6.1 Conclusion 
BloodLink provides a web-based platform for centralising emergency blood requests and donor 
coordination. It allows hospitals to create blood requests, identifies compatible, eligible, and 
available donors using stored coordinates and Haversine distance with progressive radius 
expansion, ranks donors using a priority score, and notifies them through in-app notifications. 
Donors can respond to requests, hospitals can select a response and complete the request, and 
completed donations are recorded in the donation history. 
The system is implemented with React.js, Node.js, Express.js, and PostgreSQL, and uses JWT 
authentication, bcrypt password hashing, and role-based authorisation for donors, hospitals, 
blood banks, and administrators. Indexing, bounding-box filtering, and load testing were used 
to evaluate the efficiency of nearby-donor matching. The work provides a structured approach 
to emergency blood donor coordination and a foundation for the enhancements described 
below.
